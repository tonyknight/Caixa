# Phase 1.5 - Mbox Requirements

## Overview

Mail comes into a box in four steps, each one the owner's decision:

```
 original .mbox or .zip ──read──► CLEAN ──► [Cleaned].mbox ──► SORT ──► IMPORT ──► the box
   (never changed)                junk out        │                 chosen        People/<Person>/Mail/<Account>/
                                                  │                 conversations + summaries
                                                  └────────── REDUCE ◄────┘
                                                     [Reduced].mbox: what was not imported
```

1. **Read.** Caixa reads an `.mbox` (or a `.zip` holding one) into an index: where each mail starts
   and ends, its headers and its thread, never its body in memory. The owner fills in the
   **account** the mail comes from.
2. **Clean** (optional, recommended for a first import). Caixa finds low-value mail and shows it by
   sender, grouped by why it was flagged. The owner rescues anything that matters and Caixa writes
   `(YYYY-MM-DD) <name> [Cleaned].mbox` beside the source.
3. **Sort.** The senders of the mbox (usually the Cleaned one) are listed: people already in the
   box as **Known** and chosen to begin with, everyone else **To sort**. The owner chooses or
   ignores them.
4. **Import.** The chosen senders' conversations come into the box: one page of cards per
   conversation, attachments in `Attachments/`, the facts of every mail in the master database, a
   30–50-word summary written by a local model, and one entry per conversation on each person's
   page that opens the conversation in an overlay.
5. **Reduce.** Caixa writes `(YYYY-MM-DD) <name> [Reduced].mbox` with every mail that was not
   imported, ignored senders included, so the mail can be sorted again later.

This replaces Phase 1's mail collector, which imported every mail in an mbox.

## Goals

- Read an mbox of any size, zipped or not, with progress, in bounded memory, using up to 12
  workers.
- Clean obvious junk out of an mbox aggressively, without losing receipts and orders, mail from
  the people in the box, or anything the owner wrote or answered.
- Sort senders in a window with the index page's way of working, and import only the
  conversations chosen.
- Show every imported conversation on the person's page as one entry with a local summary, opening
  the full conversation in an overlay.
- Keep what was not imported in a Reduced mbox that can be sorted again, and remember the owner's
  decisions between rounds.

## Non-goals

- Live mail accounts (IMAP, the Gmail API); other mail formats (Maildir, PST, folders of `.eml`).
- Sending, replying to or editing mail.
- Storing a conversation as Markdown or as a separate mbox in the box (decided 2026-11-20: the
  original export is the full record).
- Deleting or changing the original export.
- The personal model (Stage 2): the summaries are for people reading the box.
- Chattr's own `chattr-cli convert mail` keeps working as it does today; it does not gain cleaning,
  sorting or summaries.

## Architecture

### Components

| Component | Where | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mail index** | `caixa/mail/index.py` | Reads an mbox (or each `.mbox` in a `.zip`) once, splitting it at mail boundaries into ranges decoded by up to 12 worker processes. Records each mail's byte range, a SHA-256 of its bytes, its headers and its thread in `.caixa/mail/index.sqlite`. A zip is read as one stream that feeds the workers (a compressed member cannot be split). Bodies are parsed only where a step needs them (unsubscribe links, previews, import). |
| **Junk classifier** | `caixa/mail/clean.py` | Gives each mail the reasons it looks like junk and the reasons it must be kept (see *Cleaning rules*), rolls them up by sender, and puts each sender in one group. |
| **Senders and decisions** | `caixa/mail/senders.py` | The owner's addresses for each account, Known matching against the box's people, the threads that come with a chosen sender, and the owner's decisions (keep, choose, ignore), remembered in `.caixa/owner.json`. |
| **Mbox writer** | `caixa/mail/write.py` | Writes a Cleaned or Reduced mbox by copying the byte ranges of the mails it keeps, in their original order and byte for byte, to a `.part` file that is renamed only after every mail in it has been checked against the index. The source is opened read-only. |
| **Import** | `caixa/mail/importing.py` and Chattr's mail conversion | Copies the chosen conversations' mails into a small working mbox and imports it through Chattr's mail conversion (which decodes, files attachments and stores messages in `chattr.sqlite`), extended with the account and the new filing rules. The master's adapter then upserts every mail by Message-ID, with its account, into the new mail tables. Runs as a job with progress, cancel and undo. |
| **Conversation pages** | Chattr's renderer (`pages.py`, a new `conversation.html` template) | One HTML page of cards per conversation, in the person's `Mail/<Account>/` folder, readable on its own and in the overlay. |
| **Person page entry and overlay** | `page.html`, a new page script `conversation.js` | One entry per conversation on every involved person's page; the overlay is a dialog holding the conversation page in a frame. The script's hash joins the box pages' Content-Security-Policy, and pages drawn with an older version get the current script, as for `index.js` and `lightbox.js`. |
| **Summaries** | `caixa/summaries.py` | Asks the configured OpenAI-compatible endpoint for a 30–50-word summary of each conversation, as a job stage after the import; stored in the master. Shares the HTTP client and the "a service on the internet needs a second yes" rule with translation. |
| **Clean mail and Sort mail windows** | `web/src` (React), opened in a window of their own | One shared design: a **categorized list** of what parsing the mbox found, to be deleted (cleaning) or added (import), each item and each category with its number of mails and the space they take on disk. The index page's way of working (categories on the left, select, Shift-select, drag onto a category, tools on hover, a status bar with counts and actions, confirmation before anything is deleted, Nord colours). Reached from the Mail card in Import. |
| **Service and command line** | `caixa/service.py` (`mail.*`), `caixa/cli.py` (`caixa mail …`) | Every step in the windows is also a service method and a command. `caixa import mail` and the Mail entry of the import wizard's collector list go. |

### The mbox family and its names

| File | Written by | Holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `All mail Including Spam and Trash.mbox` (or the `.zip` holding it) | the export | everything; read only, never changed or deleted by Caixa |
| `(2026-11-20) All mail Including Spam and Trash [Cleaned].mbox` | Clean | every mail but the junk the owner confirmed |
| `(2026-11-22) All mail Including Spam and Trash [Reduced].mbox` | Reduce | every mail of its source that was not imported, ignored senders included |

- Written in the source's own folder (beside the `.zip` for a zipped source). If that folder cannot
  be written, the step stops and says so before any work is done.
- A source that already has a date and a tag loses them in the new name: cleaning or reducing
  `(2026-11-20) X [Cleaned].mbox` gives `(2026-11-22) X [Reduced].mbox`. A second file of the same
  name on the same day gets ` (2)` before the extension.
- The index remembers each file's parent and account, so a Cleaned or Reduced mbox read later is
  recognised, its account is filled in, and its decisions come back.
- Sorting a zipped mbox that was not cleaned first means Caixa unzips its `.mbox` once, beside the
  zip (sorting needs to jump to single mails, which a compressed member does not allow). Cleaning
  reads a zip directly.

### Cleaning rules

A mail is **flagged** when it has any of:

- a mailing-list header (`List-Id`, `List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Post`) or `Precedence: bulk|list|junk`;
- `Auto-Submitted` other than `no`, or a bulk sender's markers (`Feedback-ID`, a mass-mail service's
  own headers);
- a sender like `noreply`, `no-reply`, `donotreply`, `notifications`, `mailer-daemon`, `bounce`;
- an unsubscribe link in its body (English and the other languages of the fixture family, at least
  Portuguese and Spanish);
- Gmail's `Spam` or `Trash` label, or Gmail's `Category Promotions`, `Updates`, `Social` or `Forums`.

A mail is **kept whatever its flags** when:

- its sender is the owner, or a person in the box (by address, or by a name the box knows);
- the owner ever wrote to its sender, or wrote in its thread;
- it looks like a **receipt or an order** (subjects and bodies about orders, receipts, invoices,
  payments, shipping, bookings and tickets, in the same languages);
- its sender was rescued with **Keep**.

The Clean mail window groups senders under **Lists and newsletters**, **Notices and no-reply**,
**Unsubscribe links**, **Spam and Trash** (a sender goes in the group of its strongest reason), and
shows the protected ones under **Receipts and orders (kept)**. Each sender is a row: **sender name,
sender address, number of mails, total size of those mails**. Writing the Cleaned mbox drops the
flagged mails of every sender not kept.

**The list, in both windows** (decided 2026-11-20). Cleaning and importing both parse the mbox and
present items: to delete in cleaning, to add in an import. Both show them the same way: a
categorized list in which every category says how many senders and mails it holds and how much space
they take on disk, every row gives the sender's name and address, their number of mails and the space
those mails take, and the status bar adds up the selection (for example "38 senders · 12,410 mails ·
1.8 GB selected").

### Sorting rules

- **Known**: a sender whose address is an alias of a person in the box, or whose name is a person's
  name. Known senders matched by address are chosen to begin with; a match by name only is shown
  under Known, marked as matched by name, and is not chosen until the owner confirms it (decided
  2026-11-20).
- **To sort**: every other sender, the ones the owner wrote to first, then the busiest. Typing a name
  under a sender (with suggestions of the box's people, as on the index page) links the address to
  that person and makes the sender Known.
- **To import** (chosen), **Ignored**, **Imported** (already in the box from an earlier round).
- Rows as in cleaning: name, address, number of mails, total size. A preview shows a sender's mails
  before deciding.
- **What a chosen sender brings:** every thread they wrote in, whole; every thread in which the owner
  wrote to them, even unanswered. In a mailing-list thread only the mails of people in the box and of
  the owner come in.
- **Ignored** senders stay in the Reduced mbox; the decision is remembered and they start in Ignored
  the next time.
- **The owner's addresses** for an account are found from the mail labelled `Sent` (and the most
  frequent `From` of mail without labels) and confirmed by the owner when the account is filled in.

### What the box holds

```
People/Ana Costa/
  Ana Costa.html                                        her page: one entry per conversation
  Mail/<Account>/
    (2019-11-02) Fotos de São Martinho.html             one page of cards per conversation
    (2019-11-02) Fotos de São Martinho (2).html          a second conversation, same day and subject
    Attachments/
      (2019-11-02) fotos.zip
      (2019-11-02) image001.png
      (2019-11-02) image001 (2).png                      same day and name, different content
```

- **The account** is a name the owner types when an mbox is read (for example `Gmail` or `Work`). It
  names the folder and the pill `@<Account>` on the person's page.
- **A conversation's home** is the folder of the chosen person with the most mails in it (the first
  chosen, on a tie). Every other person of the box in it gets the same entry on their page, linking
  to that page. Mail the owner sent that was never answered lives with the person it was sent to.
- **Attachments** are named `(YYYY-MM-DD) <original file name>` by the date of the mail that
  carried them. The same content is stored once and linked from every card that carries it; a
  different file with a name already used that day gets ` (2)`, ` (3)` before its extension. Mail
  attachments are never converted.
- **Signature images and tracking pixels.** Images of one pixel are dropped. A small inline image
  that a sender repeats is a signature as soon as it appears a second time (decided 2026-11-20: one
  repeat is enough): the same file name and pixel size from the same sender, even when its bytes
  differ a little. It is kept once and linked from every card. The size under which an inline
  image counts as small is set on the first real mbox.
- **The conversation page**: cards in date order. Each card has the subject on its top border; then
  the date and time with the sender's offset, the sender's name and address, and the recipients'
  names and addresses (To and Cc); then the body; then its attachments (pictures shown, other files
  as links). Every header of the mail is kept in a closed **All headers** fold on its card. The page
  uses the box's Nord palette (light and dark), scrolls inside the overlay, needs no script, and works
  opened straight from disk. **How the body is drawn** (decided 2026-11-21, after the rendering trial on five real mails): the
  **mail's own HTML, made safe** (`caixa.mail.render.original`): its styles kept; scripts, event
  handlers, `javascript:`, frames, objects, forms and everything loaded from the internet taken out;
  shown in a frame that runs nothing (`<iframe sandbox srcdoc>`), its height estimated from the mail,
  pictures sent inside the mail embedded in it. A mail with no HTML part is drawn as plain text.
- **The entry on the person's page**, at the date of the conversation's first mail: the pill
  `@<Account>`, who began it, the subject, the summary (marked as written by a model, with its name),
  "12 mails · 3 files · to 2019-12-01", and **Read the conversation**. The overlay closes with
  Escape, the cross or a click beside it, and offers to open the page in a window of its own.
- The top-level `Mail/` folder and Phase 1's per-thread mail pages go away; the index page's Mail view
  lists conversations by account.
- **Every source files attachments in an `Attachments/` folder** (decided 2026-11-20, in this
  project). For mail that is `Mail/<Account>/Attachments/`; for the other sources
  `People/<Person>/<Source>/Attachments/` (and the same under `Groups/`). Boxes already built are moved
  once (ticket 14).

### Data model

**The mail index**, `.caixa/mail/index.sqlite` (Caixa's working file, readable only by its owner,
rebuildable from the mbox):

- `sources`: path, kind (`original`, `cleaned`, `reduced`), parent source, account, size,
  modification time, a hash of its first megabyte, when it was read, number of mails.
- `mails`: source, position, byte offset and length, SHA-256 of its bytes, Message-ID, In-Reply-To,
  the root of its References, Gmail thread id, thread key, time (UTC and offset), sender address and
  name, To and Cc, subject, Gmail labels, total size, number of attachments, flags and protections,
  whether the owner sent it.
- `senders`: address, names seen, number of mails, total size, first and last date, how often the
  owner wrote to them, Known person and how they were matched, group.

**The owner's preference file**, `.caixa/owner.json`, gains a `mail` section: accounts and the
owner's addresses in each; senders kept in cleaning; senders ignored in sorting. It outlives a
reset, like the rest of the file.

**The master database**, migration 4:

- `accounts`: id, name (unique).
- `mail`: one row per imported mail, keyed by its item id (`items.id`, from the Message-ID alone, or
  a hash of its headers when it has none): account, Message-ID, thread key, date (UTC and offset),
  sender address and name, recipients (names and addresses), subject. The plain text is the item's
  `text`. A mail met again in a second account is not duplicated: `mail_accounts` records each
  account it was seen in; its page stays with the first.
- `mail_threads`: thread key, account, subject, first and last date, number of mails and files, home
  person, page path, summary, the model that wrote it, when, and a hash of the mails it summarized.

### Key flows

**Clean.** Read (workers) → classify (workers; bodies parsed only for unsubscribe links and receipts)
→ the Clean mail window → the owner keeps senders → write `[Cleaned]` (one writer) → check every mail
(workers) → rename into place → index the new file as a `cleaned` child.

**Sort and import.** Read or reopen → Known, To sort, … → the owner chooses → import preview (senders,
conversations, mails, files, size) → import job: working mbox → Chattr's mail conversion (workers for
decoding and attachments) → master upsert → conversation pages → person pages redrawn → summaries
stage → done.

**Reduce.** After an import has finished, the finish step writes `[Reduced]` from the mbox that was
sorted, without every mail imported from it, and checks it as for cleaning.

**Summaries.** For each conversation without a summary, or whose mails changed: quoted history and
signatures removed, the mails in date order (within a size budget: the first mails and the last
when long) → the endpoint, asked for 30–50 words in the conversation's own language, using only what
the mails say → the answer counted (one retry, then cut at a sentence) → stored → the page redrawn.
Without a model, the entry shows the opening words of the first mail and the summary is added later.

### Workers

- A box setting **Workers**: the computer's number of processors, at most 12.
- Parallel: reading and classifying (processes over ranges of the mbox; one reader feeding them for
  a zip), checking a written mbox, decoding and filing attachments at import, drawing conversation
  pages.
- One at a time: writing an mbox (it is written in order).
- Summaries: as many requests at once as the endpoint takes (one for a model on this computer,
  four for a service), as for translation.

### Hot fixes from the first real mbox (approved 2026-11-21)

The maintainer's first use of the mail import asked for six changes, added to this project as
tickets 16–21 on the same branch.

- **Select all.** Every list in Clean mail and Sort mail has a Select all checkbox in its header
  (and Ctrl/Cmd-A): every row of that list is selected, also rows scrolled out of view; a click on
  a row then deselects it; the status bar says how many are selected and the actions apply to them.
- **Feedback from the click.** Importing first counts what the choice brings (`mail.import.preview`),
  which takes long on a large mbox. From the moment Import is clicked a progress meter stands beside
  the button: "Counting…", then the import's stages with n of N and the time taken; the button is
  disabled until the job has ended. Clean mail's Write button does the same.
- **Switches for each import.** Every import (the import wizard and Sort mail's Import dialog) has
  a **Translate** switch, and a mail import a **Summarize** switch, preset from Settings and
  changeable for that job (job options `translate`, `summarize`). Off, that job translates or
  summarizes nothing; a later job with it on catches up. An import that wrote no summaries says why
  in its result (off in Settings, off for this job, no endpoint, the endpoint failed).
- **Apple Intelligence.** Apple's on-device model (Foundation Models: macOS 26 or later, Apple
  silicon, Apple Intelligence on) can write summaries and translations instead of an endpoint. It is
  reached through a small Swift helper in `tools/apple-intelligence/`, built by Caixa the first time
  it is chosen (`swift build -c release`, which needs Xcode or its command-line tools; when that
  fails Caixa says which command to run). The helper answers `--check` (ready, or why not: not a
  supported Mac, Apple Intelligence off, the model not ready) and otherwise reads one JSON request
  per line on its input (`{id, instructions, prompt}`) and writes one answer per line
  (`{id, text}` or `{id, error, refused}`). Caixa runs as many helpers at once as the **Workers**
  setting, as a provider beside the OpenAI-compatible client, so summaries and translation use
  either. Settings → Summaries and → Translation each choose **Apple Intelligence (on this Mac)** or
  **An endpoint**; the Apple choice is offered only when `--check` says ready, and Test works for
  both. The on-device model reads about 4,000 tokens, so a conversation sent to it is held to 6,000
  characters (its first and last mails). A conversation the model's safety filter declines keeps
  its opening words and is counted. Whether several helpers are faster than one is measured on the
  maintainer's Mac (1 against 4 workers).
- **Subcategories.** Family, Friends, Business and Acquaintances on the people page (the box's index
  page as Caixa opens it) each have a folding chevron; unfolded, their subcategories are listed in
  alphabetical order with counts, and a centered + at the bottom of the list adds one. A
  subcategory can have subcategories of its own, which cannot (two levels below a category). Double
  click renames one; deleting one moves its people (and its own subcategories' people) up to its
  parent. People are sorted into a subcategory as into a category (drag, or the buttons); a
  category's count and view include its subcategories. Stored in the master database (table
  `subcategories`: id, category, parent, name; `people.subcategory_id`), kept like categories
  through a reset. Opened from disk, subcategories show and filter but cannot be changed.

## Requirements

- **M1 Read.** `.mbox` files, folders holding them, and `.zip` files holding them are read into the
  index with progress and cancel; memory does not grow with the size of the mbox; reading again a
  file already read (same size, time and first-megabyte hash) is instant.
- **M2 Account.** Reading asks for the account (a required name); Cleaned and Reduced files get their
  parent's. The owner's addresses for the account are proposed and confirmed.
- **M3 Workers.** Reading, classifying, checking and importing use up to the Workers setting (at most
  12) processes; the result is the same whatever the number of workers.
- **M4 Classify.** Every mail gets its flags and protections by the *Cleaning rules*; senders are
  grouped with their name, address, number of mails and total size.
- **M5 Clean mail window.** The groups, rows, selection, Keep, preview and status bar described
  above; writing asks for confirmation and says how many mails and how much space are left out.
- **M6 Write.** Cleaned and Reduced files are named and placed as in *The mbox family*, copied byte
  for byte, checked mail by mail before they are renamed into place; the source is never opened for
  writing.
- **M7 Sort mail window.** Known, To sort, To import, Ignored, Imported with the rules above; naming
  an address links it to a person; preview; the status bar shows what an import would bring.
- **M8 Decisions.** Keeps, ignores, accounts and the owner's addresses are kept in `owner.json` and
  come back when a Cleaned or Reduced mbox is read again, and after a reset.
- **M9 Import.** Only the conversations that come with the chosen senders are imported, as a job with
  preview, progress, cancel and undo; mail is identified by Message-ID, so importing again adds only
  what is new; the master holds the mail facts and plain text.
- **M10 Files.** Conversation pages and attachments are filed and named as in *What the box holds*,
  with the collision, signature and tracking-pixel rules.
- **M11 Person page.** One entry per conversation, on every involved person's page, with the
  `@<Account>` pill; the overlay shows the conversation page, from Caixa and from disk.
- **M12 Summaries.** Settings has a Summaries section (on or off, endpoint defaulting to
  translation's, model, "allow a service on the internet", Test with a fixed synthetic
  conversation). Summaries are 30–50 words, in the conversation's language, written after the
  import with progress, resumed where they stopped, redone when a conversation gains mails, and
  stored with the model's name and the date.
- **M13 Reduce.** After an import, the Reduced mbox holds exactly the mails of its source that were
  not imported; reading it again shows the imported senders under Imported and the ignored under
  Ignored.
- **M14 Retire the blind import.** The import wizard's Mail card opens Clean mail and Sort mail;
  `caixa import mail` is replaced by `caixa mail read | clean | keep | senders | choose | ignore |
  import | reduce`; the worker protocol's description lists the `mail.*` methods.
- **M15 Attachments folders.** Every source files its attachments in an `Attachments/` folder
  (`Mail/<Account>/Attachments/`, `<Source>/Attachments/`); a box built before this is moved once,
  restartably, with both databases' paths updated, its pages redrawn and no broken link left.
- **M16 Select all.** Every list in Clean mail and Sort mail can select all its rows at once and
  then deselect rows one by one.
- **M17 Import feedback.** From the click, a progress meter beside Import (and Clean's Write) shows
  counting and then the job's stages; the button is disabled until the job ends.
- **M18 Switches for each import.** Translate (every import) and Summarize (mail) are chosen per
  job, preset from Settings; an import that wrote no summaries says why.
- **M19 Apple Intelligence.** On a Mac whose on-device model is ready, summaries and translation
  can use it through the helper, several at once, chosen in Settings with a working Test.
- **M20 Subcategories.** The four categories have subcategories two levels deep, created with +,
  renamed and deleted, with people sorted into them and counted with their category.
- **N1 Local first.** No mail text leaves the computer unless the owner allowed a service on the
  internet for summaries; the windows load nothing from the network.
- **N2 Privacy.** The index and `owner.json` stay in `.caixa/`, readable only by the owner; `caixa
  doctor` reports nothing from them but counts.
- **N3 Safety.** Mail HTML is never shown with its scripts, event handlers or remote content,
  whichever way bodies are drawn.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The synthetic noisy mbox, plain and zipped, is read with 1 and with 12 workers to the same
      index; a generated mbox of several gigabytes is read with memory staying under 500 MB.
- [ ] Cleaning the synthetic mbox flags every list, newsletter, notice, no-reply, unsubscribe and
      Spam/Trash mail in it, and keeps every receipt and order, every mail from a person in the box,
      every mail the owner sent and every thread the owner wrote in.
- [ ] The Clean mail window lists senders by group with name, address, number and size; Keep rescues
      a sender; writing produces `(date) <name> [Cleaned].mbox` beside the source, whose mails are
      byte-identical to the kept ones and in the same order; the source's bytes and time are
      unchanged.
- [ ] The Sort mail window puts the box's people under Known (chosen when matched by address),
      lists the others under To sort with the owner's correspondents first, and naming an address
      joins it to a person.
- [ ] Importing two chosen senders brings their threads (whole), the owner's unanswered mail to
      them, and from a list thread only the mails of box people and the owner; nothing else.
- [ ] Each conversation has one page of cards in `People/<Person>/Mail/<Account>/` (subject on the
      top border, date, sender and recipients by name and address, body, attachments, a closed All
      headers fold); attachments are in `Attachments/` named `(YYYY-MM-DD) <name>`, the same content
      once, a clash numbered, a repeated signature image once, tracking pixels gone.
- [ ] The person's page shows one entry per conversation with `@<Account>`, the summary and its
      counts; Read the conversation opens the overlay in Caixa and from disk, with no
      Content-Security-Policy error; another person in the conversation links to the same page.
- [ ] The master holds every imported mail's date, sender address and name, account, subject and
      plain text, and each conversation's summary with its model and date; importing the same mail
      again (also from another account) adds nothing.
- [ ] With a stand-in endpoint, summaries are 30–50 words, follow the conversation's language, are
      resumed after a cancel, and are redone only for conversations that gained mails; without an
      endpoint the import completes and the entries show the opening words.
- [ ] Reducing after an import writes `(date) <name> [Reduced].mbox` with exactly the mails not
      imported (checked mail by mail), ignored senders included; reading it again shows Imported and
      Ignored as they were.
- [ ] Both windows show a categorized list in which every category and every row gives its number

## Risks and assumptions

- **Cleaning removes something that mattered.** Receipts usually come from no-reply addresses with
  unsubscribe links, and some friends write through newsletter tools. Mitigations: the protections,
  the owner's review by sender with previews, Keep, and the original export never changed.
- **Mail HTML** can be ugly when tidied and unsafe when not; the spike (8) decides on real mail, and
  N3 holds either way.
- **Gmail Takeout details** (`X-GM-THRID`, labels and categories, `From ` escaping, very large
  files) are still from documentation; the first real mbox may bring fixes, as Phase 1's real
  exports did.
- **Python's processes** have a start-up and copying cost; ranges must be large enough that 12
  workers are actually faster (checked by the scale test).
- **Disk space.** A Cleaned copy beside a multi-gigabyte source, and an unzipped copy for sorting a
  zip that was not cleaned, need room; the steps check free space first and say how much they need.
- **Thread keys can change** between exports (a later one has the root mail or Gmail's thread id);
  mail is keyed by Message-ID so nothing is duplicated, but a conversation could split. Merging
  conversations that share mails is part of ticket 9.
- **Undo after Reduce.** Undoing an import after its Reduced mbox was written leaves those mails only
  in the source it was reduced from; the finish dialog says so.
- **Summaries from small local models** may be wrong or too long; they are marked as written by a
  model, checked for length, and the full conversation is one tap away. Summarizing thousands of
  conversations on a laptop takes hours; it runs as a resumable stage and never blocks the import.
- **Name-only matches** can join the wrong person; they are shown as such and not chosen to begin
  with.
- **Moving existing attachments** (ticket 14) touches every box already built; it must be
  restartable and leave no broken link.
- **Apple Intelligence** cannot be built or run in the development container; its Swift code is
  written from Apple's documentation and first built on the maintainer's Mac, which may need fixes.
  The on-device model is small: summaries and translations may be weaker than a larger model's, its
  context is short, and its safety filter may decline some mail. Several helpers share one model,
  so the speed-up is unknown until measured.
