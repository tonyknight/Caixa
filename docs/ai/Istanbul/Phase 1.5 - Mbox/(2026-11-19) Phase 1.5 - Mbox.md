# Project

## Background

Phase 1 (`Istanbul/Phase1-Collector`, released as v0.5.0) built Caixa's message collectors, the
box, the master database and Caixa's own window. Mail was one of its six collectors: it reads an
`.mbox` and imports every mail in it, one page per thread under `Mail/<year>/`. It has never been
tried on a real mailbox, and that is where this project starts: a real mailbox is mostly noise. A
Gmail Takeout of a long-lived account holds tens or hundreds of thousands of mails, and most of
them are newsletters, receipts, notifications, mailing lists and strangers. Importing all of it
buries the few hundred conversations a family would want to keep: the letters between a person and
the people in their life.

Mail is worth having. For many people it is the longest written record of their life, older than
any messaging app. This project makes mail a deliberate import instead of a blind one: Caixa reads
the mbox, shows who wrote, and the owner chooses whose conversations come into the box. Everything
else stays behind, in a new and smaller mbox that can be sorted again later.

An mbox is the first way mail gets into Caixa (Gmail Takeout, Apple Mail, Thunderbird and most
other mail programs can export one). In this project **"box" always means a Caixa box**; the mail
file is **"the mbox"**.

### What Phase 1 left for mail

- `chattr.parsers.mbox_parser.MboxParser` decodes headers and charsets, keeps plain and HTML bodies,
  keeps the sender's own UTC offset, threads by Gmail thread id or the `References` chain, and
  stores attachments once by content hash.
- It reads the whole mbox into memory before it threads it, which will not do for a Takeout of many
  gigabytes.
- Its review noted that a thread's key can change between exports, so the master should key mail
  by Message-ID alone; that the HTML of a mail is stored but not shown; and that Gmail's real
  Takeout details were taken from documentation, not from a real export.
- Mail pages are drawn under `Mail/<year>/`; a person's page lists the threads they are in.
- The index page's authoring tools (categories, tiles, select and drag, name with suggestions,
  remove with confirmation), the OpenAI-compatible client used for translation, the owner's
  preference file (`.caixa/owner.json`) and import undo all exist and can be built on.

## How it should work (the maintainer's plan, 2026-11-19)

1. **Read the mbox without importing it.** Caixa indexes the mbox and lists the people who wrote:
   every sender, by address and name.
2. **Known people first.** A sender whose address or name matches a person already in the box goes
   into a **Known** category: the owner will almost certainly want their mail, so it is chosen to
   begin with.
3. **Everyone else, the owner decides.** For every other sender Caixa keeps what it knows about
   them, and the owner selects them for import or ignores them.
4. **Import whole conversations.** For each sender chosen, Caixa imports the mail they sent and
   every reply in the same threads.
5. **A short summary on the person's page.** A local AI model summarizes each conversation in 30
   to 50 words. The summary appears on the person's page as a mail entry.
6. **The full conversation, one tap away.** The whole thread is saved, with its attachments, in a
   Markdown file. Tapping the entry opens an overlay over the page that shows the whole thread in
   chronological order.
7. **What is left becomes a new mbox.** Once the chosen conversations are in the box, the mails
   imported are taken out and a new mbox is made with only the remainder, so the owner can process
   it again later without meeting what was already imported.

**Design language.** The workflow should feel like the authoring tools on the box's index page
(people as tiles in categories, tools on hover, click and Shift-click to select, drag onto a
category, a name field with suggestions, a status bar with the counts and the actions, a
confirmation before anything is deleted, the Nord colours), which the maintainer finds excellent and
fun to use, and not like the collecting side of Caixa's window (the import wizard and the jobs
list).

### Decided in discussion (2026-11-19 and 2026-11-20)

The plan above was refined with the maintainer before the Requirements were written. Where the two
differ, this list wins:

- **Cleaning comes first.** An optional pass finds low-value mail (mailing lists, newsletters,
  bulk mail, no-reply senders, mail with an unsubscribe link, Gmail's Spam and Trash) without
  removing receipts and orders, lets the owner review it by sender, and writes a slimmer copy:
  `(YYYY-MM-DD) <original name> [Cleaned].mbox`. Importing from a Cleaned mbox then writes
  `(YYYY-MM-DD) <original name> [Reduced].mbox` without the mail imported. Both are written in the
  source's own folder; the original is never changed. Junk is deleted only in the Cleaned copy;
  ignored senders stay in a Reduced copy and the decision is remembered.
- **No Markdown and no per-conversation mbox.** Each conversation is one HTML page of cards (subject
  on the top border, then date, sender and recipients by name and address, then the body), one card
  per mail in date order, with the headers kept in a fold. The original mbox stays the full record.
- **Where mail lives.** `People/<Person>/Mail/<Account>/`, attachments in an `Attachments/` folder
  beside the pages, named `(YYYY-MM-DD) <original file name>`. Signature images are kept once and
  linked. Every import source should file attachments in an `Attachments/` folder.
- **The account** is a field filled in at import. The person's page shows mail with a pill of
  `@<Account>`.
- **The database** keeps each mail's date, sender address and name, account, subject, plain text,
  and the conversation's summary.
- **One entry per conversation** on the person's page, with its summary. A conversation with
  several people in the box is kept once and linked from each person's page. Mail the owner sent to
  a known person who never answered also appears, as sent by the owner. From a mailing list, only
  the mails of people in the box and of the owner come in.
- **Summaries** are written in each conversation's own language.
- **The window** shows senders as a list (name, address, number of mails, total size) under
  categories, with the index page's way of working. Cleaning and sorting use the same format.
- **How mail bodies look** is decided after trying a small real mbox, not in advance.
- **Up to 12 workers** for the work that can run in parallel (reading, cleaning, importing).
- **The MBOX Viewer** is not used as a base (it rereads the whole file on every request, listens on
  every network interface, loads Bootstrap from the internet and shows raw MIME); only its idea of
  a list with a reader over it is kept.

**A starting point offered.** The maintainer found an open-source mbox viewer that runs as a small
web service ("MBOX Viewer", Apache-2.0, attached to the planning session of 2026-11-19) and offered
it as a possible starting point. Its assessment is part of the design discussion.

## Goals

- Read an mbox of any size (a Gmail Takeout of many gigabytes, zipped or not) into an index
  without importing anything, with progress, and without holding the mail in memory.
- Clean an mbox of obvious junk into a slimmer copy, without losing receipts, orders or anything
  from the people who matter.
- Show the senders in a window that works like the index page's authoring tools: Known people
  chosen to begin with, everyone else to be selected or ignored.
- Import only the chosen senders' conversations: their mail and the replies in the same threads.
- Put a 30–50-word summary of each conversation, written by a local model, on the person's page;
  keep the full thread and its attachments as a page of cards; open it in an overlay over the
  person's page.
- Write what was not imported to a new, Reduced mbox, so the mail can be sorted again later without
  the conversations already imported.
- Keep Caixa's promises: local first, dates first and people second, import again safely, and
  everything the window does also available as a `caixa` command.

## Non-goals

- Live mail accounts (IMAP, the Gmail API). This project reads exported mbox files.
- Sending, replying to or editing mail. Caixa is an archive.
- Photos, home videos, Obsidian vaults and PDFs: the other Stage 1.5 subjects, each its own project.
- The personal model (Stage 2). The summaries here are for people reading the box.

## Target users

- **The owner building their box.** Not technical. Has a mail export from Google Takeout or a mail
  program, and wants the mail that matters without wading through the rest.
- **The family who inherits the box.** Reads a person's page and wants to see what they wrote to
  each other, at a glance and in full.

## User stories

- As the owner, I want to see everyone who wrote to me, so that I can find the people who matter in
  a mailbox of thousands of senders.
- As the owner, I want the people already in my box recognized and chosen for me, so that I do not
  have to find them again.
- As the owner, I want to select or ignore senders as easily as I sort people on the index page,
  so that sorting a big mailbox is quick, and even fun.
- As the owner, I want only the conversations I chose imported, whole, with their attachments.
- As the owner, I want what I did not import left in a new mbox, so that I can come back to it
  later and find only what I have not dealt with.
- As someone reading the box, I want a short summary of each mail conversation on the person's
  page and the whole thread one tap away, so that I can follow a correspondence without opening
  every mail.

## Constraints

- **Local first.** Summaries are written by a model on this computer. A service on the internet is
  used only after a second, explicit yes, as for translation, because mail text would leave the
  computer.
- **The original export is never changed or deleted by Caixa** (Caixa's rule since Phase 1). It
  is the full record of every mail; the Cleaned and Reduced copies are new files beside it.
- **Very large inputs.** Memory must not grow with the size of the mbox; reading and writing show
  progress and can be cancelled.
- **Import again safely.** Sorting a remainder or a newer export again must not duplicate anything
  already in the box.
- **One service layer.** Everything the window does is also a `caixa` command; the window reaches
  Python only through the service layer.
- **This computer only.** The window is served by `caixa serve` on 127.0.0.1 under the existing
  sign-in and Content-Security-Policy rules, with nothing loaded from the network.
- **Synthetic data only.** Tests use the synthetic fixture family; no real mail in the repository.
- **Platforms.** macOS is the target; Linux is where Caixa is developed and tested.

## Success criteria

- The maintainer reads a real mailbox (a Gmail Takeout) into the index and sorts it in the new
  window.
- Known people are found and chosen to begin with, and the noise is easy to leave behind.
- The chosen conversations appear on people's pages with summaries written on this computer, and
  open in the overlay with their attachments.
- A Cleaned mbox holds no obvious junk and still holds the receipts and the people who matter; a
  Reduced mbox holds exactly the mails that were not imported (checked mail by mail), and sorting
  it again shows nothing that is already in the box.
- The project's verify command passes.
