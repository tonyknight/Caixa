# Project

## Background

Phase 1 (`Istanbul/Phase1-Collector`, v0.5.0) built Caixa's message collectors, the box, the master
database and Caixa's own window. Phase 1.5 - Mbox (v0.5.1) made mail a deliberate import with local
summaries. Both were about people writing to each other. This project adds the box's first domain
that is not a conversation: **documents**, starting with **PDF**.

A life leaves paper: bank statements, cheques, bills, invoices and receipts, insurance policies,
tax forms, contracts, deeds, medical records and letters. Today most of it is a PDF: downloaded
from a bank, printed to PDF, or scanned at home. These files sit in Downloads folders under names
like `download.pdf` or `scan_00482.pdf`, often with no text a computer can search, and they are
the first thing an heir needs to find.

The goal: Caixa takes in PDFs from the owner and leaves the originals where they are. It makes an
archival copy in the box with a usable text layer, checked page by page before it is accepted. It
works out what each document is, who issued it and when, from the filename, the text and the PDF's
own properties, with a model on this computer. It names and files the copy by date and author. A
person can find it again by searching its words or by browsing year and author pages of cards,
each card opening the PDF in an overlay.

The maintainer's draft specification, *Caixa Documents: PDF Import, Archival, Normalization and AI
Classification* (2026-11-24), is the source of this briefing. Its decisions and the changes agreed
in conversation are carried into the Requirements file.

### What Caixa already has for this

- **The collector contract** (`caixa/collectors/base.py`): preview, run, progress, undo and run
  again, with the import wizard drawn from each collector's declaration.
- **The master database** (`caixa/schema.py`, migrations 1–5), where mail added its own tables.
- **Undo snapshots** (`caixa/undo.py`), which also follow files that move (`RunResult.moved`).
- **A local model**: Apple Intelligence through the Swift helper, or any OpenAI-compatible endpoint
  such as LM Studio (`caixa/summaries.py`, `caixa/apple_intelligence.py`). Automatic picks Apple
  first, and `caixa benchmark` compares the two.
- **Box pages drawn from the master**: the index page with categories, a Mail view, calendar heat
  maps, tiles, and the conversation overlay (`conversation.js`). Caixa's window serves them under a
  strict Content-Security-Policy.
- **What is missing**:
  - Caixa has no search index of any kind; Stage 2 was to bring one.
  - Caixa has no PDF library.
  - The box's web server sends PDFs as downloads.

## Goals

- Import PDFs from a file, a folder (recursive) or a zip, with preview, progress, cancel and undo.
  Never move, change or delete the originals, and import the same file again without a duplicate.
- Give every archived PDF a searchable text layer where text is visibly present. Use OCR only on
  pages that need it, and keep the visible document unchanged.
- Validate every processed PDF (structure, content, appearance) before it is accepted, and never
  lose a document to a failed step.
- Work out each document's type, title, date, author, tags and a short summary, with a model on this
  computer. Keep the filename's evidence, record where every fact came from, and flag conflicts for
  review.
- Name and file archived copies by a standard, documented convention that later file collectors can
  reuse: `Documents/PDF/<YYYY>/<Author>/(date) Title - Author.pdf`.
- Find documents by their words (full-text, by page), by their facts (type, date, author, tags,
  status) and by meaning (semantic search and questions answered with page citations).
- Browse documents in the box: a Documents ▸ PDF view with a heat map and year range, year and
  author pages of cards, each card opening the PDF in an overlay; review and correction when Caixa
  is running.
- Link documents to the people of the box who wrote them or are named in them.

## Non-goals

- A separate document-management application, or an accounting system.
- Converting PDFs to HTML, or drawing them with a viewer of Caixa's own: the browser's viewer shows
  them.
- Moving, changing or deleting the owner's original files.
- Sending document contents to any service on the internet, AI or otherwise.
- Extracting every financial transaction, linking cheques to bills, or financial reports (the
  draft's phase 7, kept for a later project).
- Other document formats (Word, spreadsheets, presentations, text) in this project; the domain is
  designed for them.
- Requiring a model or OCR for an import to succeed.

## Target users

- **The owner**, who has years of PDFs scattered across folders and wants them in the box, named
  sensibly, without sorting each one by hand.
- **An heir or family member** who opens the box, with or without Caixa running, and needs to find
  "the house deed", "the 2019 tax return" or "every statement from that bank".

## User stories

- As the owner, I point Caixa at my Downloads folder, see how many PDFs it found, how many it has
  already, and how many need OCR, and import them without any file moving from where it was.
- As the owner, I import a scan that has no text and later find it by a word printed on its third
  page.
- As the owner, I see a document Caixa could not attribute under Unrecognized, type its author, and
  watch it move to that author's folder under its new name.
- As the owner, I review a document whose filename says one year and whose text says another, and
  choose which is right.
- As an heir, I open the box from a USB drive without Caixa, go to Documents ▸ PDF ▸ 2019 ▸ the
  insurer, and read the policy.
- As an heir with Caixa running, I ask "when did we renew the house insurance?" and get an answer
  that cites the document and page.

## Constraints

- Local only: OCR and models run on this computer; documents are never sent to a non-local
  endpoint.
- Tools: **PyMuPDF** for reading, rendering and writing PDFs (AGPL-3.0, combined with Caixa's
  GPL-3.0-or-later and stated in NOTICE). **OCRmyPDF** with Tesseract (and Ghostscript) for OCR,
  installed by the owner (Homebrew on a Mac), with `caixa doctor` saying what is missing.
- The box stays useful without Caixa: plain folders, PDFs that open anywhere, static pages.
- Synthetic data only in the repository: a synthetic PDF family, never a real document.
- The maintainer's Mac (macOS 26, Apple silicon, Safari) is where real data, Apple Intelligence and
  Safari are tried; the development container runs Linux and Chromium.

## Success criteria

- The maintainer imports a real folder of PDFs, finds them filed by year and author with sensible
  names, and finds scanned ones by their words.
- No original file is changed, and no archived PDF looks different from its source.
- Every document Caixa could not name confidently is in To review, not misnamed silently.
- Search and the overlay work in Safari on the maintainer's Mac.
